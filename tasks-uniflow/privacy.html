1Introduction

Tasks - Uniflow ("we," "our," or "us") is committed to protecting your privacy. This Privacy Policy explains how we handle your information when you use our app on iPhone, iPad or Mac, and this website. Tasks - Uniflow has no user accounts and no backend that stores your tasks. It collects nothing on our own servers except feedback and crash reports you choose to send from the app (Section 2.6).

2Information We Collect

2.1 Your Tasks and Projects

  • Tasks: title, notes, link, due date, reminder time, priority, repeat rule, completion status, and the project, section and list each task belongs to
  • Projects: name, color, symbol, notes, target date, sections and whether a project is archived
  • Where they live: in a local database on your device, using Apple's SwiftData. The database sits in a container that only Tasks, its widgets and its share extension can read.
  • Tasks from Notion and Apple Reminders are stored in the same database, so they show in one list and work offline

2.2 iCloud Sync

  • What syncs: When you're signed in to iCloud, your tasks, projects and project sections, including the copies of Notion and Reminders tasks, sync through your own private iCloud database using Apple's CloudKit. We can't see or access that database.
  • A sync identifier: Each installation of the app creates a random identifier. It's attached to a task while a change is waiting to sync, so two of your devices don't push the same change to Notion or Reminders. It isn't linked to you or your device's hardware.
  • Settings: Badge on or off, badge time, daily summary and default due date sync through iCloud key-value storage, so they match on all your devices. Every other setting stays on the device where you set it.
  • Turning it off: Turn off iCloud for Tasks in your device's iCloud settings and the app keeps working on that device alone
  • When something changes on another of your devices, iCloud sends a silent notification through Apple's push service so the app can sync. We run no push server and send no notifications of our own.

2.3 Notion (Optional)

  • Connecting: You create an internal integration in Notion and paste its token and your database ID into Settings > Notion. We never see your Notion password or account details.
  • Credentials: The token and database ID are stored in the iOS Keychain on that device only. They're not synced to iCloud or your other devices, so you connect Notion on each device separately.
  • What's exchanged: The app talks directly to Notion's API (api.notion.com) from your device. It reads your database's properties and its tasks, and writes the Task name, Done status, Notes (or Details), URL, Due Date and Priority of tasks you create or edit. Notion receives your IP address with each request.
  • Deleting: Deleting a Notion task in the app moves its page to Notion's trash. Restoring it from Recently Deleted restores the page.
  • Disconnecting: Disconnect in Settings > Notion removes your token and database ID and clears synced Notion tasks from that device. Your tasks in Notion itself are not affected.
  • The app only reaches pages you've shared with your integration. You can remove that access in Notion at any time.

2.4 Apple Reminders (Optional)

  • Permission: With your permission, Tasks uses Apple's EventKit for full access to Reminders. iOS or macOS asks you first.
  • What it does: The app reads the reminders in the lists you choose in Settings > Apple Reminders, and creates, edits, completes or deletes reminders when you do that in Tasks. All of this happens on your device. Reminders then syncs through your iCloud under Apple's privacy policy.
  • Revoking: Turn off access any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > Reminders (on Mac, System Settings > Privacy & Security > Reminders)

2.5 Device Information and On-Device Metrics

  • App version and build, system version and device model, read on your device and included with feedback and crash reports you send
  • Launch, hang and crash figures that Apple's MetricKit gives the app about this device. They're kept on your device and shown in Settings > Diagnostics. They leave your device only if you copy them yourself.
  • Details of a crash that Apple's MetricKit gives the app after it happens. They're kept on your device and leave it only in a crash report you choose to send (see Crash reports in Section 2.6).

2.6 Feedback You SendNew

Tasks - Uniflow sends feedback only when you tap Send in Settings > Feedback. Nothing is sent otherwise, apart from a crash report you choose to send (see Crash reports below). We don't ask for your email address. Each submission includes:

  • Your message: the feedback type (Bug, Idea or Other), title and details you enter
  • Device details: app version and build, system version and device model. The form shows these under "Sent With Your Feedback" before you send.

Feedback never includes your tasks, projects, notes, Notion credentials or the Diagnostics figures in Section 2.5.

Your feedback goes to a service we operate on Cloudflare Workers, which files it as an issue in a private GitHub repository for triage. The service keeps a small record of each submission: the issue number, the title, its status and timestamps, and a SHA-256 hash of a random status token. This version of the app doesn't keep the token or show your feedback's status. The service uses your IP address only to limit how often submissions can be sent, and does not store it.

Crash reportsNew

If Tasks closes unexpectedly, the next time you open it you'll see an alert titled "Tasks Closed Unexpectedly" asking whether to send a crash report. Nothing is sent unless you tap Send. Don't Send discards the report, and each crash is asked about only once. A report you send includes:

  • Device details: app version and build, system version, device model and chip architecture, and whether it's a TestFlight build
  • The crash: the exception type, code and signal, the termination reason, and the name and message of the exception if the app raised one
  • Where it happened: the code addresses of the thread that crashed (unsymbolicated stack frames), the names and identifiers (UUIDs) of the app binaries they came from, and how many times the same crash has happened on your device

A crash report never includes your tasks, notes, projects or Notion credentials. The alert says what's in it: "the app version, your device model and system version, and where in the app the crash happened."

A crash report goes through the same Cloudflare service as feedback and becomes an issue in the same private GitHub repository. The service keeps the same small record of it as of feedback. Until you answer the alert, your device keeps up to 5 waiting reports. A report you approve that fails to send, for example while you're offline, is tried again on later launches, up to 3 attempts in total. Your device also keeps a short list of crashes it has already asked about, so it never asks twice.

2.7 Notifications and Background Refresh

  • The app badge, the optional daily summary, and reminder notifications that show a task's title are all scheduled on your device. iOS asks your permission first.
  • With Background App Refresh on, iOS can wake the app to sync with iCloud, Notion and Reminders, no more often than every 15 minutes, and when iCloud signals a change

2.8 Widgets, Share Sheet, Siri and Controls

  • Widgets: The Today's Tasks widget reads your tasks from the database on your device. Completing or snoozing a task from the widget changes it there, and the app syncs the change later. Widgets make no network requests.
  • Share sheet: A link or text you share to Tasks is saved as a task on your device. The share extension makes no network requests. The app syncs the task later.
  • Siri and Shortcuts: Add Task, What's Due Today, Show Today's Tasks and Complete Task are handled by the app on your device. Siri processes your request under Apple's privacy policy, and What's Due Today reads the titles of today's tasks back to you.
  • Control Center and Home Screen quick action: These open the app. They hold no data.

3How We Use Your Information

  • Your tasks and projects are stored and shown on your device, and synced through your own iCloud, to provide the app's features
  • Notion and Reminders data is used only to show those tasks in the app and keep them in sync in both directions
  • Feedback you send is used to triage and fix problems and to plan improvements
  • Crash reports you send are used only to find and fix the crash
  • We do not use your data for advertising, sell it, or use it to track you across apps or websites
  • Apart from feedback and crash reports you choose to send, we do not collect, transmit, or store your data on our own servers

4Data Storage and Security

4.1 Data Storage

  • Local Storage: Tasks, projects and settings are stored on your device using Apple's SwiftData and system settings storage
  • iCloud: Tasks and projects sync through your private iCloud database, governed by Apple's privacy policy
  • Keychain: Your Notion token and database ID are stored in the iOS Keychain on that device only
  • Feedback and Crash Reports: Feedback and crash reports you send are processed by a Cloudflare Worker we operate and become issues in a private GitHub repository for triage
  • No Other Developer Servers: We do not operate any servers that store your tasks, projects or credentials

4.2 Security Measures

  • All network requests the app makes (to Notion and our feedback service) use HTTPS/TLS
  • The Notion token is kept in the Keychain, not in the task database, iCloud or app settings
  • iCloud data is protected by Apple's encryption and your Apple Account
  • On-device data benefits from app sandboxing and device encryption

4.3 No Tracking or Advertising

  • Tasks - Uniflow contains no advertising and no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs
  • Tasks - Uniflow does not track you as Apple defines it, so it never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt
  • This website uses no analytics or advertising of its own

5Third-Party Services

5.1 Apple iCloud and CloudKit

  • Syncs your tasks, projects and five settings across your devices using your own iCloud account
  • Apple's privacy policy applies to data stored in iCloud: Apple Privacy Policy

5.2 Notion

  • Only if you connect it. Receives the requests described in Section 2.3, directly from your device.
  • Notion's privacy policy applies to data in your Notion workspace: Notion Privacy Policy

5.3 Apple Reminders and Siri

  • Reminders is accessed on your device through EventKit, only with your permission. Siri and Shortcuts handle the requests described in Section 2.8.
  • Apple's privacy policy applies to Reminders and Siri: Apple Privacy Policy

5.4 Feedback Processors

  • Cloudflare runs the service that receives feedback and crash reports
  • GitHub hosts the private repository where feedback and crash reports are triaged
  • These services process feedback and crash reports only on our behalf, and their privacy policies apply

5.5 This Website

  • This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Opening a task's link or sharing a task hands off to your browser or the share sheet destination you choose. What happens there is governed by those apps.

6Data Retention

  • On-Device Data: Tasks, projects and settings stay on your device until you delete them or delete the app
  • iCloud Data: Stays in your iCloud account until you delete it. Deleting a task on any of your devices deletes it from iCloud and your other devices.
  • Recently Deleted: A deleted task can be restored on the device where you deleted it for 30 days, up to the 50 most recent. After that it's removed automatically.
  • Notion Credentials: Kept in the Keychain until you disconnect Notion. iOS can keep Keychain items after an app is deleted, so disconnect Notion first if you want the token removed from the device.
  • Waiting Crash Reports: Kept on your device until you answer the crash alert, and an approved report until it's sent or has failed 3 times
  • Feedback and Crash Reports: Kept as GitHub issues in our development backlog, together with the small record described in Section 2.6
  • Apart from feedback and crash reports, we hold no server-side data

7Your Rights and Data Deletion

  • Delete Tasks: Delete any task in the app. Use Delete Permanently or Empty in Recently Deleted to remove it from the device right away.
  • Notion: Disconnect in Settings > Notion, and remove the integration's access in Notion if you no longer want it
  • Reminders: Revoke access in Settings > Privacy & Security > Reminders
  • iCloud: Turn off iCloud for Tasks in your device's iCloud settings
  • Delete Local Data: Delete the app to remove everything it stores on that device, apart from Keychain items as noted in Section 6
  • Delete Feedback: To have a feedback submission or crash report deleted, email us at the address in Section 10 with the feedback's title, or the date and app version of the crash
  • No Account to Delete: Tasks - Uniflow does not use accounts, so there is no account deletion process

8Children's Privacy

Tasks - Uniflow is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Apart from feedback and crash reports a user chooses to send, the app does not collect any personal data on our servers.

11Legal Basis (GDPR)

For users in the European Union, our legal basis for processing your data is:

  • Consent: You choose to connect Notion and grant Reminders access before any of that data is read
  • Legitimate Interest: Storing and syncing your tasks on your devices and in your own iCloud to provide the app's features
  • Consent: For feedback, which is sent only when you tap Send in the app
  • Consent: For crash reports, which are sent only when you tap Send on the crash alert

Apart from feedback and crash reports you choose to send, all data is stored on your devices and in your own accounts, under your direct control at all times.

12California Privacy Rights (CCPA)

California residents have additional rights under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA):

  • Right to Know: You can request details about what personal information we collect. The only personal information we collect on our servers is feedback and crash reports you choose to send, described in Section 2.6.
  • Right to Delete: You can delete your data in the app and by deleting the app, and request deletion of feedback or crash reports you sent, as described in Section 7
  • Right to Opt-Out: We do not sell or share personal information
  • Non-Discrimination: We will not discriminate against you for exercising your privacy rights
